overview:
  - "A platform is only as useful as its ability to surface the right content at the right moment. National Geographic Education had a substantial library of resources — articles, videos, lesson plans, encyclopedic entries, maps, and more — but the search and discovery experience wasn't keeping pace with the depth of what was available."
  - "This project focused on redesigning the resource search and library homepage to make discovery faster and more intuitive for the platform's two very different primary audiences: educators planning lessons and students doing research."
problem:
  body: "Users who knew what they were looking for often couldn't find it. Users who were browsing for inspiration didn't know where to start."
  emphasis: "The search experience didn't distinguish between intent types, and the library homepage gave every resource equal weight — making it harder, not easier, to find what was relevant."

useCases:
  - ["Educators", "Searching for resources that align to specific grade levels, subjects, and academic standards, needing to evaluate multiple results quickly before selecting the right one."]
  - ["Students", "Beginning a research project with a broad topic, needing enough guidance in the search experience to self-direct without hitting dead ends."]
  - ["Returning users", "Looking for a resource they'd found before but hadn't saved, needing enough contextual cues in the results to recognize the right one quickly."]
sections:
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "01"
    title: "User Research"
    paragraphs:
      - "I conducted research sessions with educators and students separately, since their search behaviors diverged significantly. Educators were goal-oriented and filter-dependent; students were more exploratory but also more likely to give up after a failed first search."
      - "I also analyzed search query patterns and drop-off points to identify where the current experience was losing users, and why."
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "02"
    title: "Key Insights"
    paragraphs:
      - "The most common search failure wasn't a bad query; it was a failure to refine. Users weren't applying filters — either because they didn't know they existed or because the filter UI was too buried to use quickly."
      - "Students in particular needed more scaffolding at the point of search. When a search returned no obvious match, most students left rather than tried again with different terms. The experience didn't offer enough of a foothold to keep them engaged."
      - "The library homepage was organized by content type rather than by user intent, which meant the first thing educators saw wasn't necessarily the most relevant thing to their workflow."
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "03"
    title: "Search Redesign"
    paragraphs:
      - "The core of the redesign was making filtering a first-class part of the search experience — not an afterthought tucked behind a \"more options\" toggle."
    bullets:
      - ["Persistent, visible filters", "Grade level, content type, subject, and academic standards — available before, during, and after a search without requiring a page reload."]
      - ["Guided search entry", "Suggested topic prompts for users who were starting from a blank slate, reducing the cold-start friction that was causing student drop-off."]
      - ["Relevance-ranked results", "Clear metadata (grade level, content type, reading time) visible at the card level, giving educators enough signal to make a decision without clicking into every result."]
    imageLabel: "Search results — filters, cards, ranking · 16:9"
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "04"
    title: "Library Homepage"
    paragraphs:
      - "The library homepage was reorganized around user intent rather than content taxonomy. Instead of leading with format categories, it led with curated entry points — featured topics, recently added content, and grade-level browsing — giving both educators and students a faster path to something useful."
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "05"
    title: "Outcome"
    paragraphs:
      - "The redesigned search experience contributed to measurably improved user engagement across the platform, part of the body of work recognized with the 2020 Webby Award. The modular search filter system was built to be extensible, giving the content team the ability to add new filter dimensions as the library grew without requiring a full redesign."
