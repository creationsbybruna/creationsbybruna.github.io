overview:
  - "National Geographic Education serves a broad, demanding audience: K–12 students and teachers across grade levels, subjects, and learning contexts. Individual resource pages — articles, videos, lesson plans, reference entries — needed to work equally well for a fifth grader doing a report and a high school teacher building a curriculum unit."
  - "The challenge wasn't just visual. It was information architecture: how do you design a single page template that's flexible enough to support wildly different content types, while still feeling consistent and easy to navigate for every user who lands on it?"
problem:
  body: "Resource pages had grown organically over time, resulting in inconsistent layouts, unclear content hierarchies, and a user experience that varied too much from one page to the next."
  emphasis: "Teachers struggled to quickly assess whether a resource matched their students' level. Students had difficulty orienting themselves within individual pages."
  after: "The design wasn't scaling."

sections:
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "01"
    title: "User Research"
    paragraphs:
      - "I conducted user research with both teachers and students to understand how they actually used resource pages in practice: what they looked for first, where they got confused, and what made them leave a page before finding what they needed."
      - "Sessions revealed that the two groups were using the same pages in fundamentally different ways: educators were scanning and evaluating, while students were reading and exploring. The design needed to support both modes without sacrificing either."
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "02"
    title: "Key Insights"
    paragraphs:
      - "Teachers couldn't make fast relevance decisions. Grade level, subject tags, and standards alignment were either buried or inconsistently labeled, forcing educators to read deeply before deciding whether a resource was appropriate."
      - "Students lost their place easily. Long-form content without clear visual anchors or section markers created high drop-off rates, particularly on mobile. Students also lacked vocabulary support in context — a significant gap for English language learners and younger readers."
      - "The existing page template didn't flex across content types. A short encyclopedia entry and a 2,000-word feature article were using the same layout, with predictably poor results for one of them."
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "03"
    title: "A Repeatable Component System"
    paragraphs:
      - "The solution was a modular page system — a shared set of components that could be assembled differently based on content type, while maintaining a consistent information hierarchy across every page on the platform."
    bullets:
      - ["Grade level & subject indicators", "Surfaced prominently at the top of every page. No more hunting."]
      - ["Structured content sections", "Clear visual hierarchy, making it easier to scan before committing to reading."]
      - ["Inline vocabulary support", "Key terms defined in context, reducing friction for younger and non-native readers."]
      - ["Related resources module", "Surfaced contextually at page level, keeping users moving through relevant content rather than dead-ending."]
    imageLabel: "Resource page template — desktop · 16:9"
