overview:
  - "National Geographic Education serves a broad, demanding audience: K–12 students and teachers across grade levels, subjects, and learning contexts. Individual resource pages (articles, videos, lesson plans, reference entries) needed to work equally well for a fifth grader doing a report and a high school teacher building a curriculum unit."
  - "The challenge wasn't just visual. It was information architecture: how do you design a single page template that's flexible enough to support wildly different content types, while still feeling consistent and easy to navigate for every user who lands on it?"
problem:
  body: "Resource pages had grown organically over time, resulting in inconsistent layouts, unclear content hierarchies, and a user experience that varied too much from one page to the next."
  emphasis: "Teachers struggled to quickly assess whether a resource matched their students' level. Students had difficulty orienting themselves within individual pages."
  after: "The design wasn't scaling."

  - name: "Student (Middle & High School)"

      - ["Navigation drop-off", "Once done with a resource, had no clear path to what to look at next."]
useCases:
  - ["Educators", "Building multi-day lesson plans around a topic, needing to quickly evaluate a resource's relevance, grade appropriateness, and connections to other materials."]
  - ["Students", "Using NatGeo resources as primary research sources for class projects, needing clear, scannable content with supporting visuals and vocabulary tools."]
  - ["Curriculum teams", "Looking for resources that align to specific academic standards, subjects, and grade bands."]
sections:
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "01"
    title: "User Research"
    sideBy: true
    paragraphs:
      - "I conducted user research with both teachers and students to understand how they actually used resource pages in practice: what they looked for first, where they got confused, and what made them leave a page before finding what they needed."
      - "Sessions showed that the two groups were using the same pages very differently: educators were scanning and evaluating, while students were reading and exploring. The design needed to support both without shortchanging either."
    processNote: "Process images for this phase are not available for public display. The company has not cleared these materials for release."
  - group: "Product Discovery"
    number: "02"
    title: "Key Insights"
    sideBy: true
    paragraphs:
      - "Teachers couldn't make fast relevance decisions. Grade level, subject tags, and standards alignment were either buried or inconsistently labeled, forcing educators to read deeply before deciding whether a resource was appropriate."
      - "Students lost their place easily. Long-form content without clear visual anchors or section markers created high drop-off rates, particularly on mobile. Students also lacked vocabulary support in context, which hit English language learners and younger readers hardest."
      - "The existing page template didn't flex across content types. A short encyclopedia entry and a 2,000-word feature article were using the same layout, with predictably poor results for one of them."
    processNote: "Process images for this phase are not available for public display. The company has not cleared these materials for release."
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "03"
    title: "A Repeatable Component System"
    paragraphs:
      - "The solution was a modular page system: a shared set of components that could be assembled differently based on content type, while keeping a consistent hierarchy across every page."
    bullets:
      - ["Grade level & subject indicators", "Shown prominently at the top of every page. No more hunting."]
      - ["Structured content sections", "Clear visual hierarchy, making it easier to scan before committing to reading."]
      - ["Inline vocabulary support", "Key terms defined in context, reducing friction for younger and non-native readers."]
      - ["Related resources module", "Shown at page level based on context, keeping users moving through relevant content instead of dead-ending."]
  - group: "Design Solutions"
    number: "04"
    title: "Outcome"
    paragraphs:
      - "The redesigned resource pages were recognized with a 2020 Webby Award, People's Voice Winner in Websites & Mobile Sites: Science. More importantly, the component system gave content and engineering teams something they could maintain and extend on their own, without needing a designer for every new content type."
